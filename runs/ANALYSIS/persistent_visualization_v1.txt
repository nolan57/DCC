[P1] Energy cost of persistence (sustained on demand vs one-time generation)
  One-time generation energy u_in = 1.724e+17 J/m^2 = 2.74% Mc^2/A
  Confinement zero-point energy (electrons + nucleons) = 3.44e18 J/m^2 = 54.7%% Mc^2/A (K4, route M)
  => The first cost of 'persistence': turning the total energy of a transient state (including confinement zero-point energy) from 'paid once' into 'held continuously'
  Strictly speaking, if the state does not dissipate, no power is needed; but any real confinement field has leakage/tunneling/radiative losses,
  so the substantive requirement for persistence is that the confinement field maintains the potential shape over its lifetime and the loss channels are continuously compensated.
  This is an engineering constraint rather than a new physical quantity: the boundary is upgraded from R1 (instantaneous field) to R1' (sustained field + loss compensation)

[P2] Persistence requirements vs natural timescales
  Natural timescales: neutronization tau_cap=2.96 ns | tearing t_rip=4.72 ns | sound crossing 0.33 ns
  'Persistence' must greatly exceed all three, so persistence = switch off the weak interaction (or accept neutronization) + sustained confinement + continuous energy supply
  => Persistence is not 'extending the lifetime' but 'turning a transient into a steady state'; K5 changes from a conflict to an engineering constraint

[P3] Instrument visualization: translating 'invisible to the human eye' into 'detectable frequency bands'
  Human eye: 380-750 nm = 1.65-3.26 eV
  2D plasmon (optical wavevector) hbar*w_p(q_opt) = 13.8 keV -> in X-rays/deep UV, a blind spot for the human eye
  After neutronization hbar*w_p(q_opt) = 4.1 keV -> still X-rays
  Conclusion: the characteristic optical response of this object is in the keV band, where the human eye is structurally blind; but X-ray/extreme-UV instruments sit right in the window

  Frozen state  hbar*w=13.8 keV -> lambda=0.090 nm (X/soft-X band)
  Neutronized state hbar*w=4.1 keV -> lambda=0.302 nm (X/soft-X band)

  Four instrument-detectable channels (independent of the human eye):
  (a) Reflectance spectrum R(lambda): plasmon edges in the keV band
  (b) Neutrino flux (W6: 9.7e16 J/m^2, 0.56 x u_in) -- neutrino detector
  (c) Thermal emission spectrum (if T is known) -- but adiabatic => cold => weak thermal signal
  (d) Gravitational signal (stress-energy of 3.8e18 J/m^2) -- gravitational-wave / equivalence-principle detection

[P4] Optical magnitude in the sheet-conductivity framework (assumed-background test optics, not a prediction)
  For an ultrathin film, R/T/A is determined by sigma_s(omega); sigma_s is given by the finite-density polarization tensor
  Reference magnitude: graphene sigma=e^2/4hbar -> Z0*sigma = pi*alpha = 2.3%%
  For this object E_F=73 MeV, sigma_s has nothing to do with graphene (§6.4 forbids the 2.3% default)
  The only robust conclusion: both R and T can be O(1), with plasmon structure as omega varies in the keV band
  => Whether the visible band is 'nearly transparent or highly reflective' cannot be decided a priori; one must wait for the sigma_s calculation

[P5] Boundary comparison: one-time vs persistent
  One-time (previous round): R1 field >=6e23 V/m, lifetime 3-5 ns, imaging window < light-crossing timescale
  Persistent: R1' field + continuous energy source, lifetime >> t_rip, requiring simultaneous weak suppression (or acceptance of steady-state neutronization)
           Added R5: stable energy supply (power >= zero-point energy to maintain / lifetime), R6: sustained maintainability of weak suppression
  Number of knobs: 4 -> 6 (added energy supply, sustained weak suppression)

[P6] Visualization results (under the persistent boundary)
  Human eye (if visible light is still used):
    - The zero-thickness mirror is invalid (physically unreachable); the real object is an ultrathin film with L_z extremely small but nonzero
    - If R~1: mirror-like (reflecting the environment, no color of its own); if T~1: nearly invisible; neither may be asserted a priori
    - The only thing that can be said a priori: no diffuse-reflection texture (the surface has no atomic-scale roughness structure), no chemical colors
  Instruments (X-ray/neutrino/gravity):
    - X-ray reflectance spectrum: plasmon edges in the keV band, a 'fingerprint of matter'
    - Neutrino flux: 0.56 x degeneracy energy, direct evidence of the neutronization process
    - Gravity: stress-energy of 3.8e18 J/m^2, detectable at the equivalence-principle scale
  => 'Human-eye visualization' has essentially only two degenerate limits (mirror/transparent); 'instrument visualization' is the high-information window
