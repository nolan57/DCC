[B1] what matter undergoes in sequence as the front sweeps through one layer
  Step 1 break: all interlayer bonds between this layer and its neighbours lose their domain of definition in a single dimensionality reduction.
             Not torn apart (stretching takes time), but the z direction ceases to exist -- the domain of the bonds vanishes.
             Fracture area = cross-sectional area of the layer, all at once.
  Step 2 spread: the whole layer, as a 2D rigid body, translates to its lattice site.
             **In-layer (x,y) identity -> in-layer strain = 0, all displacement is taken up between layers.**
  Step 3 place: on entering the site, it joins neighbouring sites edge-to-edge only, with no in-plane bonding.

  Key inference: the whole unfolding = 8.12e+09 SE(2) rigid translations, not one of them a deformation.
  So 'smooth' holds for the advance of the front (zero strain inside each layer),
  but fails everywhere between layers (all 8.12e+09 interfaces are severed).
  [ARGUMENT] B1_zero_strain_inside_layer              PASS   in-layer strain 0; all displacement taken up by interlayer interfaces
  [ARGUMENT] B1_front_is_delamination                 PASS   front = layer-by-layer delamination surface, neither a flattening surface nor a tearing surface

================================================================================
[Conclusion] the complete process as the front sweeps through
================================================================================
  1. Morphology: a planar delamination front advancing along the body-height direction, peeling off one atomic layer (0.215 nm) at a time.
  2. Three steps per layer: break (dimensionality reduction removes the domain of interlayer bonds) -> spread (rigid translation of the whole layer) -> place (edge-to-edge joining).
     In-layer strain is always 0; all 8.1e+09 interfaces are severed, fracture area 325 km^2.
  3. Fragments: not 8.1e9 whole slices, but 1.2e+10 pieces (the leg segments split into two per layer), areas 6e-3 to 5e-2 m^2.
  4. Time: minimum 2.2 s (any faster and atoms are scattered) -> upper limit on front speed 0.78 m/s;
     if relaxed to the minute scale, the front is a slow sweep of order 1 mm/s.
  5. Heat: heat released by cutting brings the film to 300-2000 K, atoms diffuse 10-100 nm before cooling;
     atomic-scale detail is lost, the 1 mm-scale anatomical image is intact -- this picture is "blurry at atoms, clear at anatomy".
  6. Order: free, but either 'far first, near last' or keep a transport tail; the long-film-strip layout is ruled out by the speed of light.

  In one sentence: this is not 'squashing a person into a picture', but 'peeling the person layer by layer, spreading the layers flat, and filing them in order'.
  The front is more like a layer-peeling machine plus a placement machine, with a microsecond-scale heat flash in between.
