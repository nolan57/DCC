  THE RESULT, in one paragraph:

  A1 has two halves and they behave differently.  'Dimension is a topological invariant' holds for
  ALL spaces, and it applies here: the deposit's domain and codomain are FINITE DISCRETE sets,
  dimension 0 on both sides, so the invariant is satisfied trivially at 0 = 0.  'Invariance of
  DOMAIN' -- the half that forbids an embedding, and the half the submission actually needs --
  begins 'if U is an OPEN subset of R^n', and the deposit's domain has empty interior in R^3.
  A3 does not apply (its domain is [0,1]).  A4 does not apply (its set contains a unit segment in
  every direction; a finite set contains no segment at all).  A2 DOES apply -- its hypothesis is
  cardinality, which a finite pair satisfies vacuously -- and A2 is satisfied.

  So the deposit is not forbidden by any anchor, and not permitted by any either: three are out of
  scope and the fourth is satisfied by counting.  The one that applies is the one that says
  'a bijection exists and it destroys structure', which is exactly what the deposit measures:
  cardinality exact (4,279,069,764 -> 4,279,069,764), interfaces from 0 m to 1.2e4 m.

  And the reason nothing is forbidden is the topological form of DEPOSIT-BLIND: by the time you
  have a deposit, the 3-dimensional continuum is gone.  Its dimension is 0.  There is nothing
  left for a dimension theorem to protect.

  A3 is mis-stated (1->2 described, 3->2 concluded; the finite curve is a bijection).  A4 is a
  non-sequitur (a theorem about a set's Hausdorff dimension, offered as an information bound)
  and, read literally, it contradicts the conclusion it is cited for -- see P16.

  The conclusion is RIGHT and its stated foundation is not what establishes it.  'No picture' is
