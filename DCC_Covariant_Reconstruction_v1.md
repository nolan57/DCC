## 1. Unique Metric, Coordinates, and Coordinate Gauge

Adopt the most general form of 1+1-dimensional plane-symmetric reduction (retaining two independent transverse scale factors):

$$ds^2=-N^2(t,x)\,dt^2+A^2(t,x)\,dx^2+B^2(t,x)\,dy^2+C(t,x)^2\,dz^2$$

- Coordinates $x^\mu=(t,x,y,z)$, $\partial_y=\partial_z=0$ (Killing symmetry).
- $N$ = lapse, $A,B,C$ = three spatial scale factors; $x$ = bubble-wall propagation (normal) direction, $y,z$ = transverse.
- **DCC key point**: do not impose $B=C$. Y/Z anisotropy is the object of study and must retain $B\neq C$.
- Determinant: $\sqrt{-g}=NABC$.
- Inverse metric: $g^{tt}=-N^{-2},\ g^{xx}=A^{-2},\ g^{yy}=B^{-2},\ g^{zz}=C^{-2}$.

**Coordinate gauge**: for numerical work one may fix the synchronous gauge $N=1,\ \beta^i=0$. Synchronous coordinates may develop coordinate singularities (geodesic crossing) during long-term wall evolution; the numerical stage must monitor this and be prepared to switch to 1+1 ADM (free lapse/shift). The covariant equations in this document do not depend on $N=1$.

**Orthonormal frame** (locally inertial; physical observables are read here):
$$e^{\hat t}=N\,dt,\ e^{\hat x}=A\,dx,\ e^{\hat y}=B\,dy,\ e^{\hat z}=C\,dz.$$
In the orthonormal frame, $T^{\hat i}{}_{\hat j}$ gives the physical densities and pressures, independent of the coordinate scale factors.
