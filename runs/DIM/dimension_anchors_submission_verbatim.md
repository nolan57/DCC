# Submission — verbatim (`DIM-ANCHOR`)

Received 2026-11-22 20:01 (local). Stored unedited; the ruling references it by anchor number.

> GO: Dimensional Invariance Theorem (Brouwer, 1910): Two homeomorphic spaces must have the same dimension. Therefore, a homeomorphic mapping from three-dimensional space to two-dimensional space does not exist.
>
> Cantor's Mapping (1877): A one-to-one correspondence can be established between a three-dimensional solid sphere and a two-dimensional plane, but this mapping completely destroys the relative positional relationships between points. The information is preserved, but the structure is shattered.
>
> Hilbert Space-Filling Curve: A continuous surjective mapping can be constructed from a one-dimensional interval onto a two-dimensional unit square, but it cannot be injective. Multiple three-dimensional points will inevitably map to the same two-dimensional coordinate.
>
> Geometric Measure Theory (Kakeya Conjecture, 2025): Three-dimensional omnidirectional spatial information possesses an incompressible dimensional lower bound. No matter how it is folded, it is impossible to compress the full three-dimensional information into a purely two-dimensional container.
>
> Conclusion: Under the current mathematical and physical framework, a lossless physical collapse from three dimensions to two dimensions is strictly prohibited. A one-to-one mapping into two dimensions can be a file, not a picture. Therefore, the DCC model adopts a lossless serialization route, which is physically and mathematically self-consistent.
