## 0. One-sentence conclusion

**The boundary exists and lies quite far inward; it is fully characterized by 4 independent knobs, of which 2 are hard requirements and 2 are optional.**
Once all 4 knobs are set in place, what you get is a hard science-fiction object with a **clearly written specification, computable cost, and predictable shutdown consequences** — but it is **not a "person"; it is a confined transient of ultradense npe matter**.

## 3. A hard constraint inside the target: imaging capability (a newly added, unavoidable item)

The user's target includes "human-eye visualization," which introduces an **internal** condition stricter than "being able to exist":

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| Light traversing 1 m | **3.3 ns** |
| Lifetime of this state | **3–5 ns** |

**Strictly speaking there is no "scene" to see**: before light has completed a round trip, the matter has already exploded. So for "visualization" to hold, this state **must be held down for at least tens of ns** — which turns R1 (confinement) into something "for being seen" rather than "for survival." The rationale is weaker, but more realistic: **the first duty of the confinement field is to keep the object alive long enough to be seen.**

So the real choice within the boundary is: **either keep "weak preservation" and give up "smoothness" (acknowledging it is a 3 ns irreversible process);** **or keep "smoothness" and give up "weak preservation" (which would then not be "preservation" but "evolution to equilibrium")**. All three cannot be had at once.

## 6. Beyond the boundary (three things that still cannot get in)

1. **"The person is still a person"**: at 1 m², $d_\parallel=15.4$ fm, while an atom needs 0.3 nm — a factor of $2\times10^4$ difference. The only knob that could save it is an area ≥380 km² — but that is "giving up one square meter," not "adding a person."
2. **"Smooth mirror"**: a zero-thickness perfect reflector with $L_z=0$ is a degenerate limit, physically unattainable; the real object is an ultrathin film with $L_z$ extremely small but nonzero, whose optics are stated in terms of $\sigma_s(\omega)$ rather than a "zero-thickness mirror."
3. **"Self-binding"**: $P_{\rm tot}$ is positive in all three conventions and of order $10^{16}$ N/m; gravity (70 kg) cannot save it. It is always **held down**, not standing on its own.

## 7.5 Persistence / instrumentation: how the boundary changes and how the visualization looks (added 2026-11-21)

### 7.5.1 The first cost of persistence: turning a transient into a steady state

"Persistent visualization" is not a knob for "extending the lifetime," but rather transforming the **transient** into a **steady state**.
There are three natural timescales: neutronization 2.96 ns, tearing 4.72 ns, and sound-crossing 0.33 ns. Persistence must greatly exceed all three, which means **simultaneously** switching off the weak interaction (or accepting steady-state neutronization) + continuous confinement + continuous compensation of losses.

The boundary knobs increase from 4 to **6**:

| New knob | Content |
|---|---|
| R5 Stable energy supply | The confinement field maintains the potential shape over the lifetime; leakage/tunneling/radiative losses must be continuously compensated |
| R6 Persistence of weak suppression | Weak suppression ≥$10^5$ times must be **maintainable** (not a one-time switch) |

**Persistence does not change K6** (weak preservation vs. stability are mutually exclusive); it merely upgrades "weak suppression" from a one-time action to a continuous engineering constraint.

### 7.5.2 Instrumental visualization: where the human eye is blind, instruments have a window

The characteristic optical response of this object lies in the **keV band**, where the human eye (1.65–3.26 eV) is **structurally blind**:

| State | $\hbar\omega_p(q_{\rm opt})$ | Wavelength |
|---|---|---|
| Frozen state | 13.8 keV | 0.090 nm (X/soft X) |
| Neutronized state | 4.1 keV | 0.302 nm (X/soft X) |

Thus the three independent channels of instrumental visualization carry far more information than the human eye:

1. **X-ray reflection spectrum**: the plasmon edge in the keV band is the "fingerprint of matter."
2. **Neutrino flux**: 0.56× the degeneracy energy (W6), direct evidence of the neutronization process.
3. **Gravitational signal**: the stress-energy of $3.8\times10^{18}$ J/m² is detectable at the equivalence-principle scale.

### 7.5.3 Visualization appearance (under the persistent boundary)

**Human eye (visible light)**: only two degenerate limits remain — $R\sim1$ gives a mirror (reflecting the environment, with no color of its own), while $T\sim1$ gives near invisibility; **both are prohibited from a priori assertion** before the $\sigma_s(\omega)$ calculation is completed (§6.7). The only thing that can be said a priori: there is no diffuse-reflection texture (the surface has no atomic-scale roughness) and no chemical color.

**Instruments (X-ray/neutrino/gravitational)** are the information-rich window — reflection-spectrum fingerprint, neutronization neutrino flux, and stress-energy gravitational signal. **"Human-eye visualization" has almost only two degenerate limits; "instrumental visualization" truly sees "what the matter is."**

### 7.5.4 Computational example: $\sigma_s(\omega)$ and the reflectance spectrum (first reproducible rendering)

Using the 2D Drude surface conductivity + Pauli blocking (low-frequency limit $\omega\ll E_F$), compute the surface conductivity and normal reflectance $R=|r|^2$, $r=-Z_0\sigma_s/(2+Z_0\sigma_s)$, for the frozen state ($E_F=73.37$ MeV) (§6.4, first case). Code: `src/analysis/sigma_s_rendering_v1.py`; figure: `runs/ANALYSIS/sigma_s_rendering_v1.png`.

| Frequency point | $|\sigma_s|$ | $R$ | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Visible red 1.65 eV | $1.47\times10^{1}$ S | 0.9996 | Near-mirror |
| Visible green 2.48 eV | $1.06\times10^{1}$ S | 0.9996 | Near-mirror |
| Soft X 4.1 keV | $6.93\times10^{-3}$ S | 0.63 | Neutronization fingerprint band, partially transmitting |
| X 13.8 keV | $2.06\times10^{-3}$ S | 0.13 | Frozen-state fingerprint band, predominantly transmitting |

**Three reproducible conclusions:**

1. **Effective-mass self-consistency**: $m_{\rm eff}=E_F/c^2=143.6\,m_e$, digit-for-digit consistent with $E_F/m_ec^2=143.6$ in the roadmap §3.7.
2. **Effective plasma scale $\hbar D=89.28$ eV**: one order of magnitude above visible light, so at visible frequencies $Z_0\sigma_s\sim10^4\gg2$ and the reflectance approaches 1 (near-mirror). **This is not a revival of the "zero-thickness mirror"** — the latter is the degenerate limit $L_z=0$ (§5), whereas here $R\to1$ comes from the Drude conductivity of a finite-thickness film and is a genuine frequency dependence.
3. **Reflectance drops significantly in the keV band** ($R$ falls from 1 to 0.13), forming a "transmission window" — this is precisely the physical reason instrumental visualization can "see through the surface and read the compositional fingerprint," while the human eye (eV band) sees only a near-mirror, the least informative case.

**Scope limitation (must be released with the figure)**: $\Gamma=1.0$ eV is a placeholder scattering rate; the true value is given by R1-B3 transport; in the $\omega\gtrsim E_F$ band, Pauli blocking requires replacement of the Drude extrapolation by the finite-density polarization tensor (Lindhard); this result is "specified-background test optics" (MODEL RESULT), not a DCC observational prediction.

### 7.5.5 Full instrumental visualization: frozen state vs. neutronized state comparison (v2)

The numerical Lindhard function is ill-conditioned at $q_{\rm opt}/k_F\sim3\times10^{-8}$ (a 0/0 from $f_k-f_{k+q}\to0$); instead use the analytic surface conductivity in the 2D optical limit, $\sigma_s(\omega)=i e²n/(m_{\rm eff}(\omega+i\Gamma))\times F(\omega/E_F)$ (Drude weight + smooth Pauli-blocking cutoff). Code: `src/analysis/instrument_visualization_v2.py`; figure: `runs/ANALYSIS/instrument_visualization_v2.png`.

| Frequency point | Frozen-state $R$ | Neutronized-state $R$ | $|\Delta R|$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Visible 2.48 eV | 0.9996 | 0.9987 | 0.001 (**indistinguishable to the human eye**) |
| X 4.1 keV | 0.6301 | 0.1285 | 0.50 |
| X 13.8 keV | 0.1308 | 0.0129 | 0.12 |

**Peak discriminability at $\sim2.5$ keV, physical origin**: where $|Z_0\sigma_s|$ crosses $O(1)$ ($\sigma_s\sim1/Z_0\sim2.65\times10^{-3}$ S), $r=-Z_0\sigma_s/(2+Z_0\sigma_s)$ is most sensitive to $\sigma_s$; because the two states have different $n_{2D}/E_F$, their $\sigma_s$ values differ at the same energy and are maximally separated.

**Three key conclusions:**

1. **The two states are indistinguishable in the visible band** ($|\Delta R|=0.001$) — the human eye sees a near-mirror regardless of which state it views, carrying zero information.
2. **Discriminability is greatest near X-ray 2.5 keV** ($|\Delta R|\approx0.53$) — this is the optimal band for "instrumental reading of the compositional fingerprint."
3. **The drop in $R$ in the keV band is not due to Pauli blocking**, but to the inherent $|\sigma_s|\propto1/\omega$ decrease of Drude (keV $\ll E_F$; Pauli blocking becomes significant only at $\omega\sim E_F$) — this scope note must be released with the figure to avoid misreading.
