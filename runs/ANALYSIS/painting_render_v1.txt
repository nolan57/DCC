    Each slice occupies a square tile of side 0.200 m in the plane — **laterally 1:1, no magnification**
    Tiles are arranged 90126 x 90126, filling a 18.03 km square

[3] Hard upper limit: the 2D condition locks the optical depth
  Given area A, the number of layers is N_L = V/(A d), and tau = N_L * tau_1
  2D condition E_F < E_sub  <=>  V sqrt(2 pi N)/(pi A^{3/2}) < 1
  Solving gives A >= 2.795e+08 m^2 = 280 km^2 for the configuration to be 2D
  This configuration A = 324.9 km^2: N_L = 1.00 layers, E_F/E_sub = 0.80
  Boundary A = 280 km^2: N_L = 1.16 layers — **this is the thickest allowed state**

  => tau_max = 1.16 layers x 1.57e-03 = 1.82e-03
     That is: **effective 2D reduction <=> monolayer optical depth <= 0.18%**. Not an engineering problem, but a definitional one.
     Human-eye contrast threshold 1%; even the thickest qualifying picture is only 0.18%, lower by 5.5 times.
  [CHECK   ] P3_2D_caps_optical_depth               PASS   The layer count is limited to 1.16 layers by the 2D criterion, capping tau at 1.8e-03 — the picture can never be opaque

[9] Conclusion: what this picture actually looks like
  1) Geometry: a floor 18 km square and 1.00 atomic layers thick, paved with 8.1e+09 tiles of 0.20 m;
     Each tile is a 1:1 tomogram, not magnified laterally. The 'portrait' exists only in the ordering of the tiles.
  2) Visible light: tau = 1.1e-07. In reflection, transmission, at any angle, at any magnification, it is a **uniform gray film**.
     The inter-tissue ratio can reach tens of times, but the absolute signal is 1e-7 — 5 orders of magnitude short, not merely 'faint'.
  3) Soft X 284 eV: tau = 1.2e-03, inter-tissue d tau = 2.3e-03, 2e+06 photons per pixel.
     **This is the true carrier of the picture**: a monochrome, extremely thin, but structurally complete transmission image.
  4) The 2D criterion locks the layer count within 1.16 layers, with tau capped at 0.18%.
     **The picture can never be 'as dense as in Three-Body'**: density requires thickness, and thickness is not 2D.
  5) Reading it: at 1 mm resolution 10 years / 6e+20 photons; at atomic resolution 2e14 years / 1e+34 photons.

  In one sentence: the human eye has never seen the Three-Body picture.
          It exists, with the right size, right structure, right position — but its contrast lies in a channel
          that only single-photon counters can enter, and the bandwidth of that channel is determined by the number of atoms.
